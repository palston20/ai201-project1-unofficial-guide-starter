PROTECTIVE STYLES — CURL PATTERN, DENSITY, AND BREAKAGE

OVERVIEW

Protective styles are commonly used to reduce daily manipulation and potential breakage.However, styles involving extensions can also contribute to breakage and hair loss when they prevent regular hair care or create excessive tension.Protective-style recommendations should consider both curl pattern and hair density.

RISKS OF PROTECTIVE STYLES

 Extensions such as braids, weaves, wigs, and other added-hair styles can make regular washing and conditioning more difficult. Inadequate washing and conditioning can contribute to dryness and breakage, particularly for hair prone to dryness. Breakage can increase during the removal of extensions. Tight protective styles can place tension on the hairline and contribute to thinning or traction alopecia. Hair lost during takedown may include both naturally shed hair and broken hairs. Significant amounts of hair coming out after removing a protective style should not automatically be considered normal shedding.

STYLES TO APPROACH WITH CAUTION

* Microbraids
* Sisterlocks for individuals with lower-density hair or a history of hair loss
* Glued-in weaves
* Lace-front wigs attached with glue or tape
* Styles that create excessive tension or remain installed for extended periods

PROTECTIVE STYLES BY CURL PATTERN AND DENSITY

3C/4A — NORMAL DENSITY

* These curl patterns are generally less prone to dryness compared with tighter curl patterns.
* Normal-density 3C/4A hair may have a lower risk of breakage and edge thinning while wearing extensions.
* Suggested styles include:

  * Box braids
  * Senegalese twists
  * Jumbo braids
  * Sew-in weaves
  * Wigs
  * Crochet braids
* Protective styles should still be used in moderation.

4A/4B — NORMAL DENSITY

* 4A/4B hair may be less prone to dryness than 4C hair but can still experience breakage during extended protective styles.
* Avoid keeping styles installed for excessively long periods.
* Protective styles should be selected based on the individual's curl pattern, density, and history of breakage or thinning.
