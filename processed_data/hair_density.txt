CURLSMITH — HAIR DENSITY AND CURLY-HAIR CARE

OVERVIEW
Hair density refers to the number of individual hair strands on the scalp.
Hair density is generally categorized as low, medium, or high.
Understanding hair density can help determine which products and styling techniques may best support goals such as increasing or reducing volume.

TYPES OF HAIR DENSITY
HIGH DENSITY
The scalp is generally difficult to see through the hair.
Hair may feel heavier or fuller.
Stronger hair ties may be needed for ponytails.
Volume and fullness are generally not difficult to achieve.

MEDIUM DENSITY
Falls between low and high density.
Some scalp may be visible, but the hair does not appear sparse.
Volume can generally be achieved through appropriate styling products and techniques.

LOW DENSITY
The scalp and part are more visible.
There is greater spacing between visible hair follicles.
Hair may have difficulty appearing full or voluminous.
Ponytails may have a smaller circumference and require additional wraps with hair ties.

TESTING HAIR DENSITY
Hair density can be scientifically measured by counting hairs per square inch.
At home, density can be estimated by measuring the circumference of a dry ponytail.
Suggested ponytail measurements:
High density: greater than 4 inches
Medium density: 2–3 inches
Low density: less than 2 inches
For hair that is too short to measure in a ponytail, scalp visibility and the width of the part can provide an indication of density.

DENSITY AND HAIR ROUTINES
Hair density can influence how much volume or fullness a person naturally has.
Low-density hair may benefit from techniques and products intended to create additional volume.
High-density hair may require different styling approaches because fullness and volume are already naturally present.
Product selection and styling techniques should account for individual hair characteristics rather than relying on a single routine for everyone.
