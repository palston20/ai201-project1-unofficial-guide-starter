HEAT DAMAGE AND CURL DAMAGE — COMMUNITY ADVICE

TOPIC
Heat damage, damaged curls, breakage, trimming, and recovery

OVERVIEW
Excessive heat can cause significant damage to naturally curly hair. Heat-damaged hair may lose its original curl pattern and remain straight or limp even after washing, conditioning, and treatments. Community members generally recommend stopping additional heat exposure, treating the hair, and gradually removing damaged portions through trimming or cutting.
IDENTIFYING HEAT DAMAGE
Hair that remains straight or limp after washing may indicate significant heat damage.
A noticeable difference between curly roots and permanently straight ends can be a sign that the ends have been damaged.
Heat damage can occur even after a single use of high heat, although the severity varies between individuals.
Heat protectant reduces the risk of heat damage but does not guarantee that damage will not occur.

CAN HEAT-DAMAGED CURLS BE RESTORED?
Severe heat damage that permanently changes the hair's curl pattern generally cannot be completely reversed.
Treatments such as deep conditioning, protein treatments, and bond-building products may improve the feel, strength, and manageability of damaged hair.
These treatments may not restore curls that have been permanently altered by heat.
New growth will retain the individual's natural curl pattern, while severely damaged portions may remain altered until they are trimmed away.

TRIMMING AND REMOVING DAMAGED HAIR
Removing damaged hair is commonly recommended when treatments are no longer improving the hair.
A major haircut or "big chop" can remove the damaged portions at once.
Gradual trimming can be used when someone does not want to cut off all of the damaged hair immediately.
Damaged sections can be trimmed every few months while the person continues wearing styles that disguise differences in texture.
One approach is to identify ends that remain straight while healthy hair curls and trim those damaged portions.

STYLING WHILE GROWING OUT DAMAGE
Styles that can help blend damaged and healthy textures include:
Braid-outs
Twist-outs
Roller sets
Perm-rod sets
Flexi-rod sets
Wigs with lower-tension construction
Low-tension styles may be preferable when the hair is already damaged or fragile.

TREATMENTS DISCUSSED BY THE COMMUNITY
Community members suggested several types of treatments for damaged hair:
Bond-building treatments
Protein treatments
Deep conditioning
Hair masks
Steam or heat-assisted deep conditioning
Bond-building products mentioned by commenters included Olaplex, Redken, L'Oréal, and Curlsmith products. These recommendations are based on individual community experiences and should not be treated as guaranteed solutions for reversing heat damage.

PROTEIN TREATMENTS
Protein treatments were recommended by some community members to improve the strength and elasticity of damaged hair.
Protein treatments should not necessarily be used excessively.
Too much protein can contribute to stiffness, dryness, or breakage for some hair.

HEAT-PROTECTION PRACTICES
Community recommendations for reducing future heat damage include:
Use a heat protectant before blow-drying.
Use a separate thermal protectant designed for dry hair before flat-ironing.
Apply heat protectant evenly in sections rather than spraying it over the entire head at once.
Avoid flat-ironing hair at excessively high temperatures.
Reduce the frequency of heat styling.
Avoid additional heat while recovering from existing heat damage.
Allow hair to dry partially before blow-drying rather than applying high heat to soaking-wet hair.
Use lower or moderate heat settings when possible.
Some commenters recommended using multiple heat-protection products and products rated for temperatures up to 450°F. These are community recommendations rather than universal requirements.
